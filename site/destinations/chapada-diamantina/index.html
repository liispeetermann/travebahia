Chapada Diamantina

Table-top mountains, blue caves and Brazil’s wildest waterfalls

Six hours inland from Salvador, the Chapada Diamantina is Bahia’s great outdoors: a national park of sandstone mesas, crystal-clear swimming holes, blue-lit caves and the Cachoeira da Fumaça, a waterfall so tall its water turns to mist before it lands. The old diamond-mining town of Lençóis is your base.

Top experiences

  • Morro do Pai Inácio: a short, steep climb to the most iconic sunset in the park.
  • Cachoeira da Fumaça: walk to the top and lie on the rock to peer over the edge — or do the 3-day trek to its base.
  • Poço Azul & Poço Encantado: flooded caves where, roughly April to September, sunbeams turn the water electric blue.
  • Vale do Pati: one of Brazil’s most beautiful multi-day treks, staying in local family homes.
  • Cachoeira do Buracão: swim through a canyon to the foot of a thundering waterfall.

Do you need a guide?

For most trails, yes — and it’s worth it. Many attractions require or strongly recommend an accredited local guide, trails are poorly marked, and guides support the local economy. Book through your pousada or the guide association in Lençóis.

Getting there

Overnight or day buses run from Salvador to Lençóis (about 6–7 hours). Lençóis has a small airport with limited, seasonal flights. Renting a car gives the most freedom, as attractions are spread out.

Chapada Diamantina FAQ

When is the best time to visit Chapada Diamantina?

April to October is drier and best for hiking and seeing the blue light in Poço Azul and Poço Encantado. December to March brings rain but the fullest waterfalls.

How far is Chapada Diamantina from Salvador?

Lençóis is about 400 km (250 miles) from Salvador — roughly 6–7 hours by bus or car.
