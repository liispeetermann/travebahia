Traveling Brazil by Bus: Why You Should Always Book Leito

Getting There By Liis & Gleb Updated Nov 24, 2026 5 min read

Quick answer

Buses are a good way to travel between Brazilian states. Whenever possible, book a leito seat — it’s like a small individual bed that reclines almost flat, so you can actually sleep on overnight trips. Buses stop at stations every few hours, and some stations even have showers.

  • Book leito whenever it’s available
  • Expect stops every few hours
  • Some bus stations have showers
  • Bring a warm layer for the air conditioning

Why take the bus

Brazil is huge, and long-distance buses connect almost everywhere — including routes across states. They’re a comfortable, affordable alternative to flying, especially overnight, and you get to see the countryside.

Seat classes — and why leito wins

Brazilian buses come in classes, from basic convencional and executivo to semi-leito (deep recline) and leito. Definitely go for leito whenever possible: it’s like a small individual bed where you can lie down almost flat for the trip. On a night bus it’s the difference between arriving wrecked and arriving ready for the beach.

Stops along the way

Buses stop at bus stations every few hours, so you can stretch, grab food and use the bathroom. Some bus stations even have showers — although we didn’t see anyone using them!

Our tips

  • Book ahead for leito seats; they sell out first.
  • Bring a sweater or blanket — the air conditioning can be strong.
  • Keep valuables in a small bag with you, not in the luggage hold.
  • Snacks and water make long legs easier.

Frequently asked questions

What is a leito bus seat in Brazil?

Leito is the top bus class in Brazil: a wide individual seat that reclines almost flat, like a small bed, ideal for overnight trips.

Do long-distance buses in Brazil stop for breaks?

Yes, buses stop at stations every few hours for food and bathrooms. Some stations also have showers.
