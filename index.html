Bay of All Saints

Salvador

Brazil’s first capital, where Africa, Europe and the tropics meet

Salvador is the soul of Bahia. Founded in 1549 as Brazil’s first capital, it is the most African city in the Americas — you hear it in the drums, taste it in acarajé fried in palm oil, and see it in the baroque churches and candy-colored mansions of the Pelourinho, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985. The city sits on a cliff above the Bay of All Saints, split into an Upper City and a Lower City, and most U.S. visitors start their Bahia trip here.

  • Pelourinho UNESCO center
  • Daily street drummers
  • Elevador Lacerda (R$1 (~US$0.17))
  • Bonfim ribbons

Tinharé Island

Morro de São Paulo

A car-free island of numbered beaches, natural pools and sunsets

Just south of Salvador, on the island of Tinharé, Morro de São Paulo is the Bahia beach escape Americans dream about: no cars, sandy lanes, wheelbarrow porters, a hilltop lighthouse and a string of beaches simply named First, Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth. Days revolve around the tides; nights around caipirinhas on the sand.

  • Car-free village
  • Snorkeling at Quarta Praia
  • Clay at Ponta da Pedra
  • Island boat tour

Bahia travel: quick answers

Where is Bahia, Brazil?

Bahia is a state on Brazil’s northeastern Atlantic coast. Its capital, Salvador, is the main gateway; most U.S. travelers connect through São Paulo, Rio de Janeiro, Miami or Panama City. Bahia has the longest coastline of any Brazilian state.

What are the best places to visit in Bahia?

The most popular places in Bahia are Salvador, Morro de São Paulo, Chapada Diamantina, Itacaré, Praia do Forte, Trancoso, Boipeba and the Maraú Peninsula.

Do Americans need a visa to visit Brazil?

Yes. Since April 10, 2025, U.S. citizens need an e-visa for tourism, applied for online before travel.

When is the best time to visit Bahia?

September to March is best for beaches and sunshine. April to July is the rainy season on the coast, while Chapada Diamantina is best for hiking April to October.
