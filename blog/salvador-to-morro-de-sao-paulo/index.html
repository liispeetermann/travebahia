How to Get from Salvador to Morro de São Paulo (Catamaran, Ferry or Flight)

Quick answer

Take the direct catamaran from Salvador’s Terminal Náutico (about 2.5 hours, around R$150–175 (~US$25–30) one way, departures typically around 9am, 10:30am and 2:30pm), the calmer semi-terrestre route of boat + road + speedboat via Itaparica and Valença (about 3.5–4 hours, around R$145–180 (~US$24–30)), or a small plane from Salvador airport (about 25 minutes, the priciest). On arrival you pay the TUPA island fee of R$70 (~US$12) per person. If you get seasick or the sea is rough, choose semi-terrestre or fly.

  • Catamaran: ~2.5 h, ~R$150–175 (~US$25–30), from the Terminal Náutico behind Mercado Modelo
  • Semi-terrestre: ~3.5–4 h, gentler on the stomach
  • Plane: ~25 min from SSA airport, strict luggage limits
  • TUPA island fee: R$70 (~US$12) per person (pay online or at the pier)
  • Leave Morro 6–8 hours before an international flight

Option 1: Catamaran (direct)

Several companies run catamarans and fast boats from Salvador’s Terminal Náutico (behind the Mercado Modelo in the Comércio district) straight to Morro’s pier.

  • Departures: typically around 9:00, 10:30 and 14:30 from Salvador (some operators add 8:30 and 13:00); returns from Morro around 9:00, 11:30, 13:00–13:30 and 15:00.
  • Duration: about 2 hours 30 minutes.
  • Price: around R$150–175 (~US$25–30) one way in 2026, usually including the port tax.
  • Check-in: arrive 30–40 minutes early with your passport; boats leave on time.
  • Luggage: included; soft bags are easier than hard suitcases on Morro’s sandy lanes.

Schedules change with the season and sea conditions, and sailings can be switched to the semi-terrestre route when the Navy issues a weather alert.

Seasickness: what to know

The first part of the crossing is sheltered inside the Bay of All Saints; the second half is open ocean and can be rough, especially in the windy months. If you’re prone to seasickness:

  • Take motion-sickness medication about 30 minutes before boarding.
  • Choose the morning boat, when the sea is usually calmer.
  • Sit at the back, low down, and look at the horizon.
  • Eat lightly, and skip alcohol before and during the trip.

Or skip the open sea entirely with the semi-terrestre route below.

Option 2: Semi-terrestre (boat + road + speedboat)

  1. Salvador → Itaparica island: a passenger launch from the Terminal Náutico to Mar Grande (about 40 minutes), or the big car ferry to Bom Despacho when the sea is rough.
  2. Itaparica → Valença area: van or minibus, about 2 hours.
  3. Valença → Morro: speedboat down the river and bay, about 15–25 minutes.

Total: about 3.5–4 hours from Salvador (4–5 hours from the airport). Combined tickets cost around R$145–180 (~US$24–30) in 2026 and are sold by agencies as one booking. The water sections are sheltered, so this is the comfortable choice for anyone who gets seasick.

Option 3: Fly

Small air-taxi planes connect Salvador airport (SSA) with Morro’s airstrip in about 25 minutes, with spectacular views over the islands. It’s the most expensive option and prices vary by season; baggage weight limits are strict and flights depend on the weather. It makes sense if you land in Salvador and want to be on the beach the same afternoon.

Arriving: the TUPA fee and porters

Every visitor pays the TUPA (Taxa de Uso do Patrimônio do Arquipélago), the municipal tourism and preservation fee. Since December 2025 it’s R$70 (~US$12) per person; a planned increase to R$90 (~US$15) has been postponed indefinitely. You can pay at the pier on arrival or in advance online at tupadigital.com.br. Keep the receipt.

There are no cars on the island. Wheelbarrow porters wait at the pier and will carry your luggage up the hill to your pousada — agree on the price before you set off and have cash ready.

Going back: timing your flight

Don’t cut it close. Operators recommend leaving Morro at least 6–8 hours before an international flight (at least 4 hours for the semi-terrestre to the airport, longer if the sea is rough), because boats can be delayed or rerouted. Take a morning boat on the day you fly — the last catamarans leave in the early afternoon.

Booking and money tips

  • Book catamaran seats at least a day ahead in high season (December–February, Carnival, holidays).
  • Check the cancellation policy: many operators refund up to 48 hours before departure.
  • Children under about 5 usually travel free on a lap.
  • Withdraw cash in Salvador — some kiosks and boat vendors on the island are cash-only.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the catamaran from Salvador to Morro de São Paulo?

About 2 hours 30 minutes, depending on sea conditions. Boats leave from the Terminal Náutico behind the Mercado Modelo.

How much is the catamaran to Morro de São Paulo?

Around R$150–175 (~US$25–30) one way in 2026, usually including the port tax. Prices vary by operator and season.

How much is the Morro de São Paulo tourist fee?

The TUPA fee is R$70 (~US$12) per person since December 2025. You can pay it at the pier on arrival or online in advance. A planned increase to R$90 (~US$15) has been postponed.

What is the best way to get to Morro de São Paulo if I get seasick?

Take the semi-terrestre route via Itaparica and Valença — the boat sections are in sheltered water — or fly in about 25 minutes from Salvador airport.
