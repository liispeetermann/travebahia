Quick answer

English is limited outside upscale hotels in Bahia, but a few dozen words go a long way. Learn the greetings (oi, bom dia, tudo bem?), please and thank you (por favor, obrigado/obrigada), how to ask prices (quanto custa?) and where things are (onde fica…?), plus the words for the bill, ice-cold beer and the bathroom. Brazilians are warm and patient — any attempt at Portuguese is met with a smile.

  • Men say obrigado, women say obrigada
  • “Tudo bem?” is the all-purpose hello — answer “tudo bom!”
  • “Quanto custa?” = how much? — ask before you buy
  • In Brazil, “di/de” sounds like “jee” and “ti/te” like “chee”

Why a little Portuguese matters

Brazilians speak Portuguese, not Spanish — and while some Spanish words help, the pronunciation is very different. Outside upscale hotels and tour companies, English is limited in Bahia, especially with taxi drivers, beach vendors, market stalls and small restaurants. A handful of words changes everything: people relax, prices get friendlier, and conversations start. Below are the words we used every single day, grouped by situation.

Pronunciation in two minutes

  • De / di sounds like “jee”: bom dia = “bohn JEE-ah.”
  • Te / ti sounds like “chee”: noite = “NOY-chee.”
  • R at the start of a word or rr sounds like an English “h”: Rio = “HEE-oo.”
  • Final -o sounds like “oo” and final -e like “ee.”
  • -ão is a nasal “owng”: não = “nowng.”
  • Nh = “ny” (like canyon), lh = “ly” (like million).

Our tips for getting by

  • Download Google Translate’s offline Portuguese pack and use the camera mode for menus.
  • Learn the numbers 1–10 (um, dois, três, quatro, cinco, seis, sete, oito, nove, dez) for prices.
  • Smile, slow down and use gestures — Brazilians are patient and expressive.
  • A thumbs-up is the all-purpose “thanks / OK / all good” in Brazil.
  • Don’t worry about mistakes: any attempt is appreciated.

Frequently asked questions

Do people speak English in Bahia?

English is limited outside upscale hotels and tour companies. Learning basic Portuguese greetings, numbers and phrases like “quanto custa?” (how much?) and “onde fica…?” (where is…?) makes travel much easier.

How do you say thank you in Brazilian Portuguese?

Men say “obrigado” and women say “obrigada” (oh-bree-GAH-doo / dah). The form depends on the speaker’s gender, not the listener’s.

Is Brazilian Portuguese similar to Spanish?

Some words are similar, but pronunciation is very different, so Spanish speakers can often read Portuguese but struggle to understand it spoken. Brazilians appreciate it when visitors try Portuguese rather than Spanish.

What does “oxente” mean?

“Oxente” (or “oxe”) is a typical Bahian and Northeastern exclamation of surprise, like “wow!” or “what?!”.
