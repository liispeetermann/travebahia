Morro de São Paulo

A car-free island of numbered beaches, natural pools and sunsets

Just south of Salvador, on the island of Tinharé, Morro de São Paulo is the Bahia beach escape Americans dream about: no cars, sandy lanes, wheelbarrow porters, a hilltop lighthouse and a string of beaches simply named First, Second, Third, Fourth and Fifth. Days revolve around the tides; nights around caipirinhas on the sand.

Getting there and the island fee

Take the catamaran from Salvador’s Terminal Náutico (about 2.5 hours, around R$150–175 (~US$25–30)), the calmer semi-terrestre route via Itaparica and Valença (about 3.5–4 hours), or a 25-minute flight. Every visitor pays the TUPA preservation fee of R$70 (~US$12) per person (since December 2025), at the pier or online in advance. Porters with wheelbarrows carry bags from the pier — agree on the price first. Full details in our Salvador → Morro transport guide.

The beaches, one by one

  • Primeira Praia (about 5 minutes from the village): small and lively, with gentle waves for beginner surfers.
  • Segunda Praia (about 10 minutes): the social heart — beach bars by day and luaus at night.
  • Terceira Praia (about 15 minutes): calmer, family-friendly water and natural pools at low tide; many boat tours leave from here.
  • Quarta Praia (about 25 minutes): long, wide and peaceful, with the best natural pools on very low tides.
  • Quinta Praia / Praia do Encanto (about 50 minutes at low tide): nearly empty, and the best spot for kitesurfing (June–August).
  • Gamboa: across the hill, a quiet village with cliffs of natural clay for mud baths and a lovely sunset.

The island boat tour (volta à ilha)

The classic day out: about 8 hours by speedboat around Tinharé, usually 9am to 5pm, for around R$350 (~US$59) per person. Stops include snorkeling in the Garapuá natural pools (about 45 minutes), Moreré on Boipeba (lunch stop — usually not included), the colonial town of Cairu with its 1600s Franciscan convent, and Praia Bela on Boipeba. The pools depend on low tide, so check the tide before booking. Children under 5 typically go free.

Other things to do

  • Sunset at the Forte de Tapirandu (1630s fort, free) — arrive an hour early for a spot.
  • Zip line from near the fort over Primeira Praia: about R$30–50 (~US$5–8.5), ages 7 and up.
  • Climb to the lighthouse (free) for the view over the beaches.
  • Garapuá by 4x4: about R$130 (~US$22) per person for 5 hours with stops at the pools.
  • Snorkeling and diving: snorkel trips from about R$80–120 (~US$13–20); a beginner scuba dive at Garapuá around R$300 (~US$51).
  • Sunset catamaran: about R$150–250 (~US$25–42) per person with drinks.
  • Whale watching: July to October.
  • Surf and SUP: board hire around R$30–50 (~US$5–8.5) a day, lessons R$80–150 (~US$13–25).

Nightlife

Segunda Praia is the party beach, with luaus nightly in high season and on weekends the rest of the year, plus bars with live music and sunset drinks around the village. Parties start late; weeknights are quieter outside summer.

Where to stay

Stay in the village center for value and convenience (expect hills), Segunda Praia for nightlife, Terceira for calm water and tour departures, or Quarta for quiet, spacious beachfront pousadas. Book far ahead for December–February and Carnival. Our Morro area guide compares them.

Money and practical tips

  • Bring some cash from Salvador — small kiosks, porters and boat vendors are often cash-only.
  • Pack light, in soft bags; everything is on foot over sand and hills.
  • Rain in May–July can cancel boat trips; plan buffer days.
  • Use SPF 50 and take a floating dry bag for beach days.
  • Leave Morro 6–8 hours before an international flight.

Where to stay

Where to Stay in Morro de São Paulo: Which Beach Is Right for You

From refined boutique pousadas to charming guesthouses tucked into the jungle, Morro has a stay for every style. Choose Terceira Praia for the best all-round balance of calm water, restaurants and boat-tour departures; Segunda Praia if you want nightlife on your doorstep (and don’t mind music); Quarta Praia for quiet, space and the best natural pools; the village for value and convenience; or Gamboa for total calm across the hill. Expect around R$200–700 (~US$34–120) a night for a good mid-range pousada and R$750–1,800 (~US$125–305) for high-end — more over New Year and Carnival.

Morro de São Paulo FAQ

How do I get from Salvador to Morro de São Paulo?

Take the catamaran from Salvador’s Terminal Náutico (about 2.5 hours, around R$150–175 (~US$25–30)), the semi-terrestre route via Itaparica and Valença (about 3.5–4 hours, calmer), or a small plane from Salvador airport (about 25 minutes).

How much is the Morro de São Paulo tourist fee?

The TUPA fee is R$70 (~US$12) per person since December 2025, paid at the pier or online in advance.

Which beach is best in Morro de São Paulo?

Segunda Praia is best for nightlife, Terceira Praia for families and boat tours, and Quarta Praia for long walks and the best natural pools at low tide.

How much is the island tour in Morro de São Paulo?

The full-day volta à ilha boat tour costs around R$350 (~US$59) per person, with stops at Garapuá, Moreré, Boipeba and Cairu. Lunch is usually extra.
