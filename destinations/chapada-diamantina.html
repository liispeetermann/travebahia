Chapada Diamantina

Table-top mountains, blue caves and Brazil’s wildest waterfalls

About 410 km inland from Salvador, the Chapada Diamantina is Bahia’s great outdoors: a national park of sandstone mesas, crystal-clear swimming holes, blue-lit caves and the Cachoeira da Fumaça, a waterfall so tall its water turns to mist before it lands. The old diamond-mining town of Lençóis is the easiest base.

Top experiences

  • Morro do Pai Inácio: a short, steep climb to the park’s most iconic sunset (R$20 (~US$3.5)).
  • Cachoeira da Fumaça: about 2 hours’ hike from Vale do Capão to lie on the rim and peer over a 340–380 m drop — or a 3-day trek to its base.
  • Poço Azul & Poço Encantado: flooded caves where, roughly April to September, sunbeams turn the water electric blue (around R$70 (~US$12) each).
  • Vale do Pati: one of Brazil’s most beautiful multi-day treks, staying in local family homes.
  • Cachoeira do Buracão: swim through a canyon to the foot of an 85 m waterfall (guide mandatory).
  • Gruta da Lapa Doce & Pratinha: a vast show cave and a river-and-cave swimming spot.

Where to stay

Lençóis is the best-equipped base, with the airport, agencies, restaurants and card payments. Vale do Capão suits hikers and has a relaxed, alternative vibe (no phone signal in the valley). Mucugê is cool and charming and the southern gateway to the Pati, and Ibicoara is the base for Buracão.

Fees and guides

The national park itself has no gates and no entry fee. Private attractions charge: Pai Inácio R$20 (~US$3.5), Poço Azul around R$70–100 (~US$12–17), Poço Encantado around R$70 (~US$12), Pratinha R$90–110 (~US$15–19), Lapa Doce around R$60–120 (~US$10–20), Cachoeira do Mosquito around R$60 (~US$10). Guides are mandatory at Buracão and Lapa Doce and strongly recommended elsewhere — trails are unsigned and there’s no signal. Agency day tours from Lençóis run roughly R$250–750 (~US$42–125) per person.

Getting there

Rápido Federal buses run Salvador → Lençóis around 8am, 1pm and 11pm (about 6–7 hours, roughly R$140–280 (~US$24–47) by class). Azul flies to Lençóis typically on Thursdays and Sundays. Driving takes about 6 hours via the BR-324 and BR-242; drive in daylight. There’s no Uber or public transport to trailheads.

When to go and safety

April to September is dry and best for hiking and the blue light; December to March brings fuller waterfalls, storms and flash-flood risk. If a river turns muddy or loud, leave the riverbed immediately. Nights drop to around 10°C in winter. Read our full Chapada hiking guide for itineraries, packing and safety.

Chapada Diamantina FAQ

When is the best time to visit Chapada Diamantina?

April to September is drier and best for hiking and the blue light in Poço Encantado. December to March brings rain but the fullest waterfalls.

How far is Chapada Diamantina from Salvador?

Lençóis is about 410 km (255 miles) from Salvador — roughly 6–7 hours by bus or 6 hours by car. Azul also flies to Lençóis, typically twice a week.

Is Chapada Diamantina National Park free?

Yes, the park is free to enter, but private attractions such as Poço Azul and Pratinha charge fees, and guided tours cost extra.
