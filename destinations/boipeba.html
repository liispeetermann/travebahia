Boipeba

Morro’s quieter sister — empty beaches and the Moreré pools

If Morro de São Paulo feels busy, hop south to Boipeba: a protected island of sand roads, coconut groves and some of the clearest natural pools in Bahia at Moreré. Most visitors arrive on Morro’s island tour; staying overnight lets you enjoy it after the day-trippers leave.

  • Moreré pools
  • Cueira lobster
  • Shipwreck diving
  • Car-free island

Don’t miss

  • Moreré natural pools at low tide — snorkel with tropical fish, then eat seafood in the fishing village.
  • Praia da Cueira for grilled lobster on the sand.
  • Ponta dos Castelhanos, known for diving at a historic shipwreck site.
  • Velha Boipeba, the main village, with its river beach and sunset over the mangroves.

The beaches

  • Boca da Barra: the river-mouth beach by Velha Boipeba, calm water and sunset over the mangroves.
  • Tassimirim: a long, quiet stretch of palms a short walk south of the village.
  • Cueira: wide sand, gentle surf and the famous grilled-lobster barracas.
  • Moreré: a fishing village with the island’s best natural pools offshore at low tide.
  • Bainema and Ponta dos Castelhanos: wild, nearly empty beaches at the island’s far end, best reached by boat or a long walk at low tide.

When to go and tides

September to March brings the sunniest weather; April to July is wetter and quieter. Everything on Boipeba revolves around the tide: beach walks between villages and the Moreré pools are only possible around low tide, so check the tide table before planning each day.

Practical tips

  • There are no cars — just sand roads, tractors, boats and your feet.
  • Bring enough cash: cards work at many pousadas, but small kiosks and boatmen often take cash only, and ATMs are unreliable.
  • Use repellent at dusk near the mangroves.
  • Pack light and in soft bags.

Prices are for a double room per night, usually with breakfast, based on public listings on Booking.com, Kayak and TripAdvisor and local travel guides (November 2026). Expect 1.5–3× higher rates over Christmas, New Year and Carnival.

Getting there

There’s no regular direct boat from Morro to Boipeba; most people visit on the round-the-island tour from Morro (around R$350 (~US$59)) or by private transfer. From the mainland, boats run from Valença and Torrinhas. Short local boats link Velha Boipeba with Moreré (around R$15 (~US$2.5) per person each way). A visitor fee for Boipeba (R$50 (~US$8.5)) has been announced but is currently suspended.

Details checked November 2026.

Boipeba FAQ

Is Boipeba a day trip from Morro de São Paulo?

Yes — the full-day island boat tour from Morro stops at Boipeba and Moreré. Staying a night or two lets you enjoy the island after the day-trippers leave.
