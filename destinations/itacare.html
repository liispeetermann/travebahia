Itacaré

Surf beaches tucked into the Atlantic rainforest

Itacaré is where the Atlantic rainforest crashes straight into the surf. A laid-back town with a backpacker-meets-boutique vibe, it is surrounded by jungle-trail beaches, waterfalls and the cacao farms that made southern Bahia famous.

Getting there

The nearest airport is Ilhéus (IOS), about 70 km away — roughly 1–1.5 hours by transfer, taxi, rental car or regular Rota buses along the paved coast road. From Salvador, fly to Ilhéus in about an hour, or take the ferry to Itaparica and a bus south (about 5–6 hours).

Best beaches

  • Concha: the town beach, calm water where the Rio de Contas meets the sea — good for families and paddleboarding.
  • Resende, Tiririca, Costa and Ribeira: the urban beaches south of town. Tiririca is the famous surf beach (better for experienced surfers); Ribeira has gentler waves and is where the Prainha trail starts.
  • Prainha: a small bay ringed by jungle and coconut palms, reached by a 40–60 minute trail from Ribeira — arguably the prettiest beach in the region. Only a simple kiosk; bring snacks.
  • Engenhoca, Havaizinho, Camboinha and Itacarezinho: wild beaches off the coast road, linked by the “four beaches” trail (about an hour one way between Engenhoca and Itacarezinho). Engenhoca is the best for beginner surfers.
  • Jeribucaçu: a wild beach where a river meets the sea, about 30 minutes’ walk from the road; best at low tide.

Surfing

Itacaré has waves year-round, biggest from May to September. Surf schools run lessons at Engenhoca and Ribeira for around R$80–150 (~US$13–25); board hire is around R$50 (~US$8.5) an hour. Beginners should avoid Tiririca on big days.

Waterfalls, rivers and cacao

  • Cleandro waterfall: reached only by water, paddling through mangroves on the Rio de Contas (half day).
  • Tijuípe waterfall: an 8 m roadside fall with a restaurant, about 30 minutes away (around R$20 (~US$3.5)).
  • Cacao farm tours: visit a cabruca farm where cacao grows under the rainforest canopy and taste bean-to-bar chocolate.
  • Boat day to the Maraú Peninsula: around R$200–300 (~US$34–51).

Nightlife and practical tips

The pedestrian Rua Pedro Longo is lined with restaurants and bars, with live music, forró and pagode most nights. Hike trails in daylight and never alone, use a guide or offline maps (there’s no signal on most trail beaches), and don’t leave bags unattended on remote beaches.

Itacaré FAQ

How do you get to Itacaré?

Fly to Ilhéus (IOS), about 70 km away, then take a transfer, taxi or Rota bus for about 1–1.5 hours. From Salvador, you can also take the ferry to Itaparica and a bus south.

Is Itacaré good for beginner surfers?

Yes. Engenhoca and Ribeira have mellow waves on smaller days, and many surf schools offer lessons for around R$80–150 (~US$13–25).
