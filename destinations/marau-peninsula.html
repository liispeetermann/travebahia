Maraú Peninsula

Taipu de Fora’s reef pools and a lagoon-laced wilderness

Remote and gloriously undeveloped, the Maraú Peninsula is reached by boat across the Camamu Bay to the village of Barra Grande. Its star is Taipu de Fora, a vast reef that becomes a natural aquarium at low tide — right at the shoreline.

Highlights

  • Taipu de Fora: reef pools that form right at the beach at low tide — no boat needed. Jardineira shuttles or quad bikes from Barra Grande cost around R$25 (~US$4) per person each way.
  • Barra Grande’s sandbanks: about a kilometer offshore, walkable at very low tide or by boat (around R$75–100 (~US$13–17)).
  • Lagoa do Cassange: a freshwater lagoon steps from the ocean.
  • Sunset from Ponta do Mutá at the tip of the peninsula.
  • Boat trips to Moreré and Boipeba (around R$200–250 (~US$34–42)).

More beaches on the peninsula

Beyond Taipu de Fora, the peninsula’s ocean side is a string of long, empty beaches: Taipu de Dentro, Algodões with its calm reef-protected water, Saquaíra and Três Coqueiros. The bay side faces the islands of the Camamu Bay, one of Brazil’s largest bays, with mangroves and fishing villages.

Kitesurfing and activities

Steady winds make the peninsula a kitesurfing spot, especially from around June to September. You can also take boat trips around the Camamu Bay’s islands and waterfalls, paddle in the lagoon, or simply walk for hours on empty sand.

When to go

September to March is sunniest. Plan pool days around low tide — the best Taipu de Fora conditions are on very low tides near full and new moons. December–January and Carnival are the busiest weeks in Barra Grande.

Getting there

Most travelers reach Camamu by road (from Ilhéus or Salvador) and take a speedboat across the bay to Barra Grande — about 30–40 minutes, around R$50–75 (~US$8–13) per person. A long dirt road also runs from Itacaré. Bring cash; services on the peninsula are limited.

Maraú Peninsula FAQ

How do I get to Barra Grande?

Most travelers take a speedboat from Camamu (reachable by road from Salvador or Ilhéus), about 30–40 minutes across the bay for around R$50–75 (~US$8–13). There is also a dirt road from Itacaré.
